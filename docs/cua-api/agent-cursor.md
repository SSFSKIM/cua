While you act in an app, a cursor labelled with this session's name shows the user where each action lands, unless the user has turned it off, and fades a few seconds after your last action. Your actions do not wait for it, it is not the user's pointer, and your screenshots do not show it.

A web page in a Chromium-based app (Chrome, Electron apps) or in Firefox that is in the background does not take a pointer: unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, a left click, double click or drag there is refused with "<App>'s web page does not accept a background click; use a dedicated desktop, or ask the person to bring <App> forward", and nothing is sent. A plain click there on a button, checkbox, radio button, link, pop-up button or menu item goes through the accessibility action of that element instead, and one on a checkbox or radio button whose state does not change fails with `clickUnconfirmed`. A plain click there into a text field, text area or editable region gives it keyboard focus through accessibility instead, with no mouse event, so the page sees no click; the result's text says whether the caret was put at the point, and when it was not, typing goes to the field's existing selection or its start, so use select_text or set_value first if the position matters. In Firefox the field is focused by its own accessibility action instead, which does not put the caret at the point. A field that does not report focus fails with `clickUnconfirmed`. Unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, a click on a background Firefox window outside its page, on the toolbar for instance, can fail with `clickUnconfirmed` even when it took effect, as Reload does. A click or drag that fails with `clickUnconfirmed` was sent but may only have activated the window: observe the app before any retry.

A click or drag at coordinates is checked against the screenshot the coordinates came from: when what that screenshot showed at the point is gone, has moved off it, has given way to another control, or has been relabelled (a Save button that now reads Delete), it fails with `screenChangedSinceScreenshot` and nothing is sent. Take a new screenshot and give the coordinates in it. The check reads the accessibility tree at the point, and also refuses a click or drag when the app has opened a new window over the one in the screenshot, when the point has left the page the screenshot showed, or when a web page has added an element over the point since the screenshot, such as a suggestion list or popup. A suggestion list the page shows without adding elements (one shown by opacity, or moved into place) still takes a click aimed at the control under it. In Chrome, so can a list that overflows its field's container, because Chrome's hit-test answers the covered control. Chrome's own popup of a field's `<datalist>` suggestions lets a click pass through to the page under it. The check still cannot see text that has moved within the same text view (after Return or a paste in TextEdit), a label that changed only in its numbers (a list row reused from "Order #1234" for "Order #1235"), or a toggle switched with its label unchanged. After typing into a field that offers suggestions, press Escape or take a new screenshot before clicking near it. After editing text above the point, take a new screenshot before clicking at coordinates. When a screenshot shows a panel over the page, aim at what the screenshot shows, not at the page's elements under it.

A click, scroll or drag at coordinates outside the screenshot they came from fails with `pointOutsideScreenshot` and nothing is sent: give a point inside it, from 0 to its width less one across and from 0 to its height less one down, in its pixels. So a drag cannot end outside the app's window, on another app or the desktop. A scroll succeeds only once what is at its point was seen to move, or when that was already scrolled as far as it goes that way, in which case the result's text says so. Otherwise it fails with `scrollUnconfirmed`: the scroll was sent but nothing was seen to move, so observe the app (a new screenshot or `getAXState()`) before scrolling again.

When a window holds more than its accessibility tree can list, the tree lists what the window shows first, in its usual order, and ends with a line such as `[truncated: at least 1234 elements omitted, above and below the visible area]`, the count a lower bound and the words saying which way the omitted elements lie. To reach an element the tree does not list, scroll toward it and call `getAXState()` again. A visible control inside a container that reports a collapsed frame can still be left out. In a Chromium-based app, a table's column and header elements take new element IDs at every observation.

`typeText` reads the focused field back after typing, and succeeds only once the field holds what it held before with its selection replaced by the text. Otherwise it fails with `typeUnconfirmed`: the text was sent, but the field does not read as expected, so some of it may have gone in. Observe the field with `getAXState()`, then put it right with `setValue`; do not type the text again. When the field reads other text, the error shows the expected and the actual text around the first difference; for a `{{secret:…}}` it says only that the field does not match. It fails the same way where the field cannot be read back: a password field, or a field longer than accessibility reports in full. Typing into an element that is not a text field, such as Finder's list when you type a name to select a row, also fails with `typeUnconfirmed` once the keys are sent: observe the selection to see whether it moved. macOS's own text substitutions, such as a period added after a double space, automatic capitalisation or text completion, change what was typed, so they make `typeText` fail with `typeUnconfirmed`, never succeed silently. In a multi-line field a Return is checked like any other character. A Tab, or a Return in a single-line field, is pressed only once the text before it reads back, and what it does, such as moving to the next field or submitting a form, is not confirmed; inside a `{{secret:…}}` it is sent with the rest of the secret, which is checked once, at its end. To replace a field's text, use `setValue`, or `selectText` and then `typeText`, rather than a select-all key and typing: `pressKey` answers once its keys are sent, without confirming what an editing key such as super+a, super+z or super+v did, so text typed right after it can land before the key takes effect. Under an input source that is not a Latin keyboard layout, `typeText` pastes its text instead, and this read-back does not apply.
