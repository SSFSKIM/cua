While you act in an app, a cursor labelled with this session's name shows the user where each action lands, unless the user has turned it off, and fades a few seconds after your last action. Your actions do not wait for it, it is not the user's pointer, and your screenshots do not show it.

Read an action's error before acting again. A refusal (`screenChangedSinceScreenshot`, `appWindowOverPoint`, `pointOutsideScreenshot`, `backgroundClickRefused`, `windowOnAnotherSpace`, or a web page that does not accept a background click) sent nothing. An error ending in `Unconfirmed` (`clickUnconfirmed`, `scrollUnconfirmed`, `menuUnconfirmed`, `valueUnconfirmed`, `typeUnconfirmed`) means the action was sent but its effect was not seen, so it may have taken effect: observe the app (a new screenshot or `getAXState()`) before any retry, and never repeat it blindly.

A click, scroll or drag at coordinates is checked against the screenshot the coordinates came from, and must lie inside it, from 0 to its width less one across and from 0 to its height less one down, in its pixels, so a drag cannot end outside the app's window. When what that screenshot showed at the point has changed or moved, or a window or element has come over it, it fails with `screenChangedSinceScreenshot` or `appWindowOverPoint` and nothing is sent, unless it is carried by the element's own action (`macos/click-checks`): take a new screenshot and aim again, use keys, or click an element `getAXState()` lists. The check cannot see every change: after typing into a field that offers suggestions, press Escape or take a new screenshot before clicking near it; after editing text above the point, take a new screenshot before clicking at coordinates; and when a screenshot shows a panel over the page, aim at what the screenshot shows, not at the page's elements under it. A permission prompt's buttons are clicked like any other button, so a click on Allow grants the site what it asks for.

Unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, a web page in a Chromium-based app (Chrome, Electron apps) or in Firefox that is in the background does not take a pointer: a left click, double click or drag there is refused and nothing is sent, except a plain click on a control, which goes through the control's accessibility action, or into a text field, which gets keyboard focus through accessibility with no mouse event; the result's text says whether the caret was put at the point, and when it was not, use `selectText` or `setValue` first if the position matters. Unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, while an app is not frontmost a right click is refused with `backgroundClickRefused`, and so is a click on a Chromium-based app's own button that opens a menu or panel: for an element's own menu, use Show Menu (`performSecondaryAction` with "Show Menu") on that element by its ID; otherwise ask the person to bring the app to the front, or reach the command by keys, the app's menu bar or the page's elements.

To replace a field's text, use `setValue`, or `selectText` and then `typeText`, rather than a select-all key and typing. When `typeText` fails with `typeUnconfirmed`, some of the text may have gone in: observe the field with `getAXState()`, then put it right with `setValue`; do not type the text again.

For the detail, use `nodeRepl.write(await agent.documentation.get("<name>"))`; the call alone returns the text without showing it:
- `macos/background-web-pages`: how a background Chromium or Firefox page takes each click, text-field click and arrow key, and that a Chromium-based app's first observation in a task can take up to about 2.5 s longer; read before acting in a web page of an app that is not frontmost
- `macos/click-checks`: every refusal of the screenshot check, how a click on a suggestion list, permission prompt, menu item, pop-up button, combo box, menu button or colour well is carried and confirmed, and what the check cannot see; read before clicking in a menu, list or popup, or when a click is refused
- `macos/action-answers`: a scroll succeeds only once what is at its point moved or was already at its end, and a Show Menu only once a menu opened, else `scrollUnconfirmed` or `menuUnconfirmed`; read when one of them or `setValue` fails, or before `setValue` in a background Chromium page, whose field then gets no focus event
- `macos/accessibility-tree`: a tree ending `[truncated: …]` lists what the window shows first, so scroll toward an element it omits and observe again; a `[window off screen: …]` line or `windowOnAnotherSpace` needs the user to move the window onto a display or switch to its Space; a Chromium-based app's table columns and headers take new element IDs at every observation; read when an element you need is not listed
- `macos/typing`: how `typeText` confirms its text, a password field checked by its length or caret, input methods that paste, and a Tab or Return in the text; read before typing a `{{secret:…}}`, into a password field, or under a non-Latin input source, and when `typeText` fails
