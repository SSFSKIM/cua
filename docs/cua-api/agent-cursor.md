While you act in an app, a cursor labelled with this session's name shows the user where each action lands, unless the user has turned it off, and fades a few seconds after your last action. Your actions do not wait for it, it is not the user's pointer, and your screenshots do not show it.

A web page in a Chromium-based app (Chrome, Electron apps) or in Firefox that is in the background does not take a pointer: unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, a left click, double click or drag there is refused with "<App>'s web page does not accept a background click; bring <App> forward or use a dedicated desktop", and nothing is sent. A plain click there on a button, checkbox, radio button, link, pop-up button or menu item goes through the accessibility action of that element instead, and one on a checkbox or radio button whose state does not change fails with `clickUnconfirmed`. A plain click there into a text field, text area or editable region gives it keyboard focus through accessibility instead, with no mouse event, so the page sees no click; the result's text says whether the caret was put at the point, and when it was not, typing goes to the field's existing selection or its start, so use select_text or set_value first if the position matters. In Firefox the field is focused by its own accessibility action instead, which does not put the caret at the point. A field that does not report focus fails with `clickUnconfirmed`. A click or drag that fails with `clickUnconfirmed` was sent but may only have activated the window: observe the app before any retry.

A click or drag at coordinates is checked against the screenshot the coordinates came from: when what that screenshot showed at the point is gone, has moved off it, or has given way to another control, it fails with `screenChangedSinceScreenshot` and nothing is sent. Take a new screenshot and give the coordinates in it.

When a window holds more than its accessibility tree can list, the tree lists what the window shows first, in its usual order, and ends with a line such as `[truncated: at least 1234 elements omitted, above and below the visible area]`, the count a lower bound and the words saying which way the omitted elements lie. To reach an element the tree does not list, scroll toward it and call `getAXState()` again. A visible control inside a container that reports a collapsed frame can still be left out. In a Chromium-based app, a table's column and header elements take new element IDs at every observation.
