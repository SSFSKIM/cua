On Linux, some calls can answer in ways the document above does not list. Each such answer says what happened; do what it says before you act again.

This desktop is either shared, the default, where a person may be using it, or dedicated to you. On a shared desktop, input that an app ignores while its window is in the background fails with `<App> does not accept background input; use a dedicated desktop or activate the window first`, and nothing is sent. Typing, keys, scrolling, drags and clicks at coordinates can fail this way; a click on an element by its index still works there. Unless the user asked you to work in that window in front, say what you could not do instead of activating it. On a dedicated desktop such input brings the window to the front by itself. A drag that starts a drag and drop on a shared desktop is cancelled before the drop, and its answer begins `<App> began a drag and drop, which changes the desktop`.

A failure that says nothing was sent sent no input: fix what it names, then you may send the request again. A failure that says a focusing click already reached the window may have moved its focus or caret: read the window before trying again. `Paste was sent once and could not be confirmed; read the field before retrying`, from `typeText` or `paste`, means the text may have arrived, now or a little later: read the field with `getAXState()`, and send the text again only if it is not there.

`listWindows()` marks a minimized window, or one on another desktop, with `minimized: true`. `getAXState()` still reads its tree, but its screenshot fails with `Screenshot unavailable for window <id>.` and input to it fails with `Window <id> is minimized; activate it first`, with nothing sent. Unless the user asked you to work in that window, say that it is minimized instead of restoring it.

`Window <id> matches several accessibility windows; close or retitle the duplicates` means that windows of one app, with one title and one size, cannot be told apart, so nothing was read or sent. Ask the user to close or retitle the duplicates; do not close their windows yourself.

A context menu requested on an element, by a right click on it or by its `showContextMenu` action, succeeds once a menu is seen to open. Otherwise it fails with `showContextMenu was sent and no menu was seen within 1 s; the application may have shown its own menu or ignored the request; read the window before retrying`: take a screenshot to see whether a menu opened before you request it again.

A window state whose first line begins `Pending from your last drag,` says that your last drag did something after it answered: a drop may have landed, or a drag and drop may have changed the desktop. Read the window before sending input again; the next input request after it is not run, and its failure says so.

An accessibility tree that ends with `Accessibility tree truncated: at least <n> elements not shown (<reason>)` lists only part of the window: the reason says whether the element limit, the depth limit or elements that could not be read cut it, and the elements under each cut are missing. Act on what it lists, use a screenshot and coordinates for what it does not, or narrow what the window shows, by closing a panel or opening only the part you need, and call `getAXState()` again.
