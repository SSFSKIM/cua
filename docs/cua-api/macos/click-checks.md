A click or drag at coordinates is checked against the screenshot the coordinates came from: when what that screenshot showed at the point is gone, has moved off it, has given way to another control, or has been relabelled (a Save button that now reads Delete), it fails with `screenChangedSinceScreenshot` and nothing is sent. Take a new screenshot and give the coordinates in it. The check reads the accessibility tree at the point, and also refuses a click or drag when the app has opened a new window over the one in the screenshot, when the point has left the page the screenshot showed, or when a web page has added an element over the point since the screenshot, such as a suggestion list or popup. A click on a row or button of a window the app itself shows over the one in the screenshot, namely one of Chrome's address-bar suggestions, a button of a site's permission prompt, or a swatch in a native app's popover, goes through that row's own accessibility action, with no click posted: it succeeds once that window has closed, and otherwise fails with `clickUnconfirmed`, so observe the app before any retry. A permission prompt's buttons are clicked like any other button, so a click on Allow grants the site what it asks for. The row is the one the screenshot showed at the point, and it is pressed only while it is still there with the same text, in the same place, and enabled. Otherwise (as when the list has changed after more typing, or the point is on a control inside the row, such as a suggestion's remove button), for a drag, a double click or another button, and on any other window the app shows over the one in the screenshot, such as Chrome's popup of a field's `<datalist>` suggestions, it fails with `appWindowOverPoint` and nothing is sent, because a click would reach the window under it: take a new screenshot and aim again, use keys instead (the arrow keys and Return to take a suggestion, Escape to close the list), or call `getAXState()` and click an element it lists. A click on an item of a menu the app shows in a window of its own (the list of a pop-up button or a `<select>`, a context menu) goes through the item's own accessibility action instead, since a click posted there would only close the menu: it succeeds once the menu has closed and the control it belongs to has moved to the item, and otherwise fails with `clickUnconfirmed` (as an item of a context menu does, having no such control, and the item already chosen), so observe the app before any retry. When the item is not the one the screenshot showed at the point, or is disabled or takes no action, the click fails with `appWindowOverPoint` and nothing is sent: use the arrow keys to the item and Return, or click the menu item `getAXState()` lists by its element. A menu whose items the accessibility tree does not list at the point (a menu-bar menu or its submenu) fails the same way, with nothing sent: a click outside it closes it, and if a fresh `getAXState()` lists the item (a menu-bar menu's items, after its title in the menu bar is clicked by its element), click the item by its element. A click on a closed pop-up button in a native app (not one of a Chromium-based app's own buttons), a web page's `<select>`, or a combo box's arrow, goes through that control's own accessibility action, which opens its menu or list, since a click posted to an app in the background can close the menu it opens: it succeeds once the menu or list has opened, and otherwise fails with `clickUnconfirmed`, so observe the app before any retry; take a new screenshot or call `getAXState()` to see its items before clicking one. A combo box whose arrow has no such action fails with `backgroundClickRefused` while the app is not frontmost, and nothing is sent: ask the person to bring the app to the front, or type the value into the combo box's text field instead. While the app is not frontmost, a click on a native app's menu button or color well, and a click on a web page's field that offers a `<datalist>` list, likewise go through the control's own accessibility action, since a click posted there can close what it opens: each succeeds once its menu, panel or list has opened, and otherwise fails with `clickUnconfirmed`; if that action brings the app to the front over the person's window, the answer says so. Unless this Mac is a dedicated desktop, while the app is not frontmost a click on a Chromium-based app's own button that opens a menu or panel (Chrome's app menu, profile, tab search or site information button) fails with `backgroundClickRefused` and nothing is sent: ask the person to bring the app to the front first, or reach the same command another way, by keys, the app's menu bar or the page's elements. So does any right click, since no accessibility action opens a context menu at a point: for an element's own menu, use Show Menu (`performSecondaryAction` with "Show Menu") on that element by its ID. A suggestion list the page shows without adding elements (one shown by opacity, or moved into place) still takes a click aimed at the control under it. In Chrome, so can a list that overflows its field's container, because Chrome's hit-test answers the covered control. The check still cannot see text that has moved within the same text view (after Return or a paste in TextEdit), a label that changed only in its numbers (a list row reused from "Order #1234" for "Order #1235"), or a toggle switched with its label unchanged. After typing into a field that offers suggestions, press Escape or take a new screenshot before clicking near it. After editing text above the point, take a new screenshot before clicking at coordinates. When a screenshot shows a panel over the page, aim at what the screenshot shows, not at the page's elements under it.
